\begin{abstract}
Large language models can generate expressive quests and dialogue, but fluent or schema-valid text does not establish that a proposed event is executable, authorized, or consistent with canonical game state. We present \system, a symbolic commit gate that treats every generated event as an untrusted transaction proposal. A type-strict parser, an externally supplied action policy, and seven deterministic checks grouped into six state-relative families decide each admitted transition; a failing candidate may enter bounded repair for at most $K+1$ recorded attempts, and a passing candidate is revalidated before mutation. The counterexample-guided operator $\rho$ reads only the prior candidate and its typed validation errors, never authoritative state. Unkeyed SHA-256 content links, state-semantic replay, and assignment-complete accounting make every terminal outcome auditable. Three evidence lanes stay separate: a deterministic offline conformance pilot over one authored world, a single-model live screening extension, and a closed-world knowledge-graph link-proposal simulation with no runtime authority. The offline lane establishes mechanism conformance only; at $K{=}1$ the live lane observed 5/5 guided versus 0/5 blind commits solely in a deliberately repairable policy-blind regime, so it remains pilot-only. No lane establishes population efficacy, model superiority, player experience, or commercial-engine performance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{I}{nteractive} generation is more than a language task. A candidate event may be natural yet require an absent item, assert a fact unknown to its speaker, reveal forbidden quest information, introduce an unauthorized effect, or regress a quest stage. Executable text environments make the distinction between a plausible utterance and a valid transition explicit \cite{S11_cote2019textworld,S12_wang2022scienceworld}. Grounded dialogue and personalized quest systems further show the value of quest, entity, and graph context \cite{S03_weir2024ontologically,S20_ashby2023personalized}, but context does not itself authorize canonical mutation. \textbf{\system{} treats generated game events as untrusted transaction proposals and permits canonical state mutation only through an external, deterministic commit gate.} Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} reads one such transaction left to right.

Structural constraints solve an adjacent problem: grammar-constrained decoding, query languages, and incremental parsers restrict generated structures \cite{S09_geng2023grammar,S10_beurerkellner2023prompting,S36_scholak2021picard}, yet a syntactically admissible event can still be false relative to the current world. Retrieval and memory supply useful evidence \cite{S04_he2024gretriever,S05_gutierrez2024hipporag,S06_chhikara2025mem0}, but retrieved content is not a state-transition authority either.

This paper presents \system, an implementation that combines these concerns as an auditable transaction protocol. Its current contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{C1---Contracts:} versioned trust-boundary contracts and a type-strict parser that rejects unknown keys and ambiguous types at proposal and replay boundaries (Sec.~\ref{sec:arch}; E1);
  \item \textbf{C2---Controller:} a validate--repair--commit controller with seven checks across six state-relative families, bounded repair, unchanged fallback, and pre-commit revalidation (Sec.~\ref{sec:repair}; E1, ENG1);
  \item \textbf{C3---Audit:} SHA-256-linked records, state-semantic replay, and episode-continuity checks (Sec.~\ref{sec:audit}; E1, ENG1);
  \item \textbf{C4---Harness:} an assignment-complete offline conformance harness that keeps a classified terminal row even when no proposal trace can exist (Sec.~\ref{sec:lanes}; E1); and
  \item \textbf{C5---Repair:} the counterexample-guided operator $\rho(a,E)$, compared with blind retry and a state-reading oracle across frozen repairability classes (Sec.~\ref{sec:repair}; E1, E2).
\end{enumerate}
Table~\ref{tab:contribution-map}, generated from the machine-checked contribution matrix, binds C1--C5 to their prior-work topics, evidence lanes, and inference ceilings; Table~\ref{tab:evidence-lanes} defines the lanes.

\section{Related Work and Research Gap}
\subsection{Interactive Narrative and Game Agents}
KNUDGE grounds branching NPC dialogue in quest and entity specifications \cite{S03_weir2024ontologically}; knowledge-graph quest generation \cite{S20_ashby2023personalized}, memory--reflection--planning agents \cite{S07_park2023generative}, and trainable role-playing agents \cite{S08_shao2023character} address complementary aspects of grounded, believable behavior. Player-facing studies evaluate preference and perceived dialogue quality \cite{S21_akoury2023player,S22_hochreiter2026beyond,S23_yin2026contextualized}, and a 2025 preprint reports role-sensitive stability--improvisation trade-offs \cite{S02_figueiredo2025scaffolded}; both motivate separating hard admissibility from optional naturalness and player-experience constructs.

Experience-driven generation \cite{S24_yannakakis2011experiencedriven}, observer-labelled engagement \cite{S25_melhart2020engagement}, and limits of vision-language engagement inference \cite{S26_wang2026engagement} motivate a future adaptation track; affect is non-authoritative and absent from the present evidence.

\subsection{Environments and Benchmarks}
TextWorld and ScienceWorld expose explicit interactive state \cite{S11_cote2019textworld,S12_wang2022scienceworld}, MineDojo adds open-ended embodied tasks \cite{S13_fan2022minedojo}, and GVGAI separates agent, game, and content tracks \cite{S14_perezliebana2019gvgai}. AgentBench, AgentBoard, SOTOPIA, and BALROG evaluate agents across heterogeneous, process-diagnosed, social, and long-horizon game settings \cite{S15_liu2024agentbench,S16_ma2024agentboard,S17_zhou2024sotopia,S18_paglieri2025balrog}; automated play-style agents complement rather than replace human evaluation \cite{S19_lepelletier2022playtesting}. None prescribes a transactional authorization boundary.

\subsection{Constraints, Neuro-Symbolic Validation, and Retrieval}
Constrained decoders improve output structure \cite{S09_geng2023grammar,S10_beurerkellner2023prompting,S36_scholak2021picard}; \system{} instead treats structure as an input condition to independent state-relative authorization. The same generate-and-verify division is established outside games: LLM-Modulo frameworks let a model propose plans that external sound critics accept or reject \cite{S50_kambhampati2024llmmodulo}, and SayCan admits a language-proposed skill only after a state-dependent affordance check \cite{S51_ichter2023saycan}. AgentSpec expresses runtime rules for LLM agents as trigger--predicate--enforcement triples \cite{S52_wang2025agentspec}; \system{} fixes the predicates to game state and adds a repair contract and replayable records. Setting the DC grounds D\&D agents in tool calls with precondition checks \cite{S53_li2025settingdc}, whereas \system{} admits free-text proposals only after parsing and external policy evaluation. STORY2GAME lets the LLM generate the action preconditions and effects it plays under \cite{S54_zhou2025story2game}; here the policy is authored independently of the proposer. \system{} instantiates the critic as a deterministic, state-relative commit gate that returns typed counterexamples. Game-specific comparators span complementary boundaries: PANGeA validates content by asking an LLM to judge and refine it against designer rules \cite{S42_buongiorno2024pangea}; IVIE, a preprint associated with non-archival ICCC 2026 material, incrementally generates interactive-fiction worlds with symbolic validation \cite{S01_vaucher2026ivie}; and a 2026 successor restricts the LLM to selecting authored world-state transformations \cite{S44_gongora2026worldstate}, gaining consistency by construction without a transactional gate, audit-linked records, or repair-operator evidence. No comparison implies superiority: validity is only validity for the predicates a system encodes.

G-Retriever, HippoRAG, and Mem0 address subgraph retrieval and long-term memory \cite{S04_he2024gretriever,S05_gutierrez2024hipporag,S06_chhikara2025mem0}; Generative Agents and Character-LLM address behavioral memory and persona \cite{S07_park2023generative,S08_shao2023character}. \system{} may consume such context but denies it commit authority; their benefits are not measured here.

\subsection{Auditability and Reproducibility}
Process metrics motivate retaining intermediate failures \cite{S16_ma2024agentboard}; robust evaluation cautions against overinterpreting sparse stochastic runs \cite{S31_agarwal2021precipice}. Reproducibility and environmental reporting motivate frozen artifacts and explicit measurement boundaries \cite{S33_pineau2021reproducibility,S34_henderson2020energy}. Human and LLM evaluation require construct, rater, and bias controls \cite{S27_vanderlee2019best,S28_karpinska2021perils,S29_zheng2023judging,S30_chen2024judgement}, while future non-inferiority and participant analyses require justified margins and random-effects structures \cite{S32_lakens2018equivalence,S35_barr2013random}. Record-and-replay is an established agent paradigm for experience reuse \cite{S56_feng2025agentrr}; \system{} claims only semantic replay of admitted state transitions.

The bounded novelty is the implemented combination, not the invention of its individual parts. Prior work separately demonstrates runtime enforcement for general agents \cite{S52_wang2025agentspec}, symbolic state management in generated game worlds \cite{S53_li2025settingdc,S54_zhou2025story2game}, feedback-driven repair \cite{S45_madaan2023selfrefine}, and agent record-and-replay \cite{S56_feng2025agentrr}; \system{} studies their intersection at a game-state commit boundary, where parsed proposals are checked against an independent policy and deterministic predicates, optionally repaired from bounded validator feedback, and recorded for semantic replay. Action interposition is long established: narrative mediation intercepts an action before it executes \cite{S37_riedl2003managing}; classical planning defines applicability through preconditions and effects \cite{S39_fikes1971strips}; runtime enforcement admits only permitted transitions \cite{S40_schneider2000enforceable}; transaction processing leaves the prior state intact on abort \cite{S49_haerder1983transaction}; authored-logic interactive drama governs which utterances a character may make \cite{S38_evans2013versu}; and belief-aware narrative planning reasons over what characters know, which our knowledge and disclosure predicates encode far more coarsely \cite{S41_ware2021sabre}. C1--C2 place strict contracts and a validate--repair--commit controller over this lineage; C3--C4 add checksum-linked records, state-semantic replay, episode continuity, and assignment-complete accounting that retains cases with no proposal trace; C5 isolates a repair operator that consumes only typed pre-execution validator errors. We do not infer that a feature absent from a report is absent from every implementation.

\section{Problem Definition and Guarantee Boundary}
For exposition, the controller input used by validation at step $t$ is decomposed as $c_t=(G_t,q_t)$, where $G_t$ denotes typed snapshot fields and $q_t$ denotes the externally authored action-policy, disclosure, and quest-stage constraints stored with that snapshot. Terminal record history $h_{<t}$ is maintained separately from the \texttt{WorldState} snapshot; no validation predicate reads it, and the implementation does not reconstruct state from the log. Retrieval, narrative scores, model rationales, memory summaries, and any future affect estimate are non-authoritative proposal context.

An untrusted proposer emits candidate $a_t$. Strict parsing first enforces the shared candidate-key contract, including rejection of unknown top-level fields, and establishes the declared schema. The validator returns
\begin{equation}
V(c_t,a_t)=(v_{\mathrm{policy}},v_{\mathrm{pre}},v_{\mathrm{reach}},
v_{\mathrm{know}},v_{\mathrm{disc}},v_{\mathrm{quest}},E_t),
\end{equation}
where $E_t$ is a structured error set. Mutation is
\begin{equation}
c_{t+1}=\begin{cases}
T(c_t,a_t), & \bigwedge_i v_i=1,\\
c_t, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

The controller asserts the following implementation invariants under its encoded contracts; the pilot tests their mechanisms with deterministic valid, invalid, repair, fallback, replay, and fault-injection fixtures rather than presenting general theorems. \textbf{I1 (exhausted-failure immutability):} if no returned candidate through budget $K$ validates, or proposal/controller execution fails before commit, the returned state equals the supplied prior state. \textbf{I2 (bounded recorded attempts):} provided every proposal and repair callback returns, repair budget $K\geq0$ admits at most $K+1$ recorded candidate attempts; a successful commit adds one defensive validation immediately before mutation. The runtime does not enforce callback deadlines, so I2 is not a wall-clock termination guarantee. \textbf{I3 (effect authorization):} an accepted candidate contains no declared fact effect in \texttt{CandidateAction.effects} outside \texttt{ActionPolicy.allowed\_effects}, and any quest-stage target is explicitly authorized by that policy. \textbf{I4 (state-semantic replay):} with the frozen schemas and deterministic transitions, an accepted trace reconstructs its stated terminal state.

These invariants assume correct policy authorship, faithful extraction into declared candidate fields, deterministic software versions, returning callbacks, and single-process file ownership. Replay revalidates the recorded structured candidate and transition; it does \emph{not} verify the provenance, prompts, or stochastic generation of intermediate repairs, and natural-language implications omitted from structured fields remain outside the guarantee.

Validation errors are exposed to a repair callback as structured error codes. Repair never directly mutates state, and exhaustion returns an unchanged deterministic fallback. Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} shows the terminal paths. The pilot compares two implemented callbacks. The \emph{counterexample-guided operator} consumes only the prior candidate $a$ and the validator's structured error set $E$, never the authoritative state:
\begin{equation}
\rho(a,E)=a \oplus \textstyle\bigcup_{e\in E}\delta(e),
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
where each $\delta(e)$ is the deterministic per-error-class edit of Table~\ref{tab:rho-rules} and $\oplus$ applies the resulting field edits. Each edit touches at most one candidate field per error entity, so the number of changed fields per attempt is bounded by $|E|$; applying $\rho$ twice with the same error set is a fixed point; and an entity simultaneously demanded and rejected on the same field is declared irreparable and left untouched. The \emph{state-reading reference callback} instead discards the error set and reconstructs the policy-governed fields (preconditions, effects, and both quest-stage fields) directly from the authoritative state; it deliberately never edits declaration-integrity fields (required objects, used facts, disclosed facts), because undeclaring a dependency or disclosure would launder the violation past the gate rather than repair the candidate. It is an oracle upper bound over state-readable repairs, and its outcomes must not be read as evidence about any deployable repair method.

\subsection{Integrity, Semantic Replay, and Accounting}\label{sec:audit}
Each result row carries an unkeyed SHA-256 \emph{integrity checksum}, and proposal outcomes link to a trace checksum; these detect accidental or tested content changes but are not signatures, writer identity, or protection against an actor who rewrites content and checksums. Semantic replay strictly parses the prior state and every recorded candidate/validation pair, requires every nonfinal attempt to be invalid, recomputes the terminal transition, compares the stated final state, and requires continuity between adjacent JSONL records; it does not re-execute the repair step that produced candidate $j+1$.

The terminal record classes are commit, symbolic fallback, adapter failure, and controller failure. A controller-failure row remains in the frozen assignment denominator and leaves state unchanged, but it may legitimately lack a completed proposal trace because no candidate outcome was produced. Accordingly, result completeness does not imply trace existence for every terminal row. The pre-execution assignment key contains run, arm, scenario, seed, model, revision, controller-configuration hash, assignment-input hash, and prior-state hash. Aggregation rejects duplicate, missing, or unexpected keys and separately reports symbolic, adapter, and controller failures.

\subsection{\textbf{E1} Offline Conformance Design}
The pilot asks whether (PQ1) valid candidates commit while named invalid-policy cases remain unchanged; (PQ2) rejection-only, blind unchanged retry, $\rho$, and the state-reading reference callback produce the expected outcome in every frozen repairability class; (PQ3) frozen checksum, state-semantic, linkage, and continuity mutations are rejected by their designated check operations and an injected second-file append failure triggers the pair-write rollback check; (PQ4) strict adapters and assignment manifests retain exactly one classified terminal row per assigned case or fail closed; and (PQ5) proposal and replay parsers both reject an otherwise-valid candidate carrying one unknown top-level key.

Cases are purposively designed conformance fixtures in four domains (validator/state isolation, repair-arm comparison, record/trace fault injection, adapter/accounting contracts), not samples from a model or game population. Every repair fixture declares a frozen repairability class before execution: \emph{guided-repairable} (the error payload alone suffices for $\rho$), \emph{oracle-only} (repair needs the authoritative state), or \emph{irreparable} (no arm may commit); each class's expected outcome per arm is part of the fixture contract, unique cases form the denominators, and all expectations precede execution.

Measures are exact counts: validator-class agreement; rejected or failed cases with state mutation; commit and attempt outcomes by arm and repairability class; detectable faults rejected by their designated operation; replayed traces; rejected manifest violations; and proposal/replay parity on the unknown-key regression, which is parser-contract parity rather than a safety outcome. Exception class alone does not identify a stable detector layer, so no detector attribution is reported, and no $p$-values or population intervals are attached. Adapter cases carry JSON-Schema-pinned synthetic latency and token constants that exercise accounting propagation only. The release packet freezes fixture and assignment manifests, schemas, the result JSON with embedded traces, environment and code revision, generated tables, and checksums, following reproducibility and measurement-boundary guidance \cite{S33_pineau2021reproducibility,S34_henderson2020energy}.

\subsection{\textbf{E2} Live RQ2 Screening}
The live extension compares blind unchanged retry with $\rho$ at matched $K{=}1$: per cell, five seed-indexed Codex CLI calls request one candidate from \texttt{gpt-5.6-sol}, and the same candidate feeds both arms, eliminating within-call proposal differences. Conditions vary policy visibility and whether a trivial zero-effect route exists. The promoted packet retains the current cells and the superseded v1 diagnostic, binds every JSON/JSONL receipt by SHA-256, and labels the evidence \texttt{screening-pilot-only}. Because seed labels do not control the hosted sampler, the revision is unpinned, token accounting is unavailable, and constructed states are not population samples, we report exact counts and failure classes without $p$-values, confidence intervals, or model ranking.

\subsection{\textbf{E3} KG/Ontology Link-Proposal Simulation}
\texttt{SL-KG-ONTOLOGY-SIM-001} is an engineering-only closed-world lane separate from the authoritative \texttt{WorldState}: 43 OKF nodes and 106 reference edges, 24 reviewed typed edges, passing type/domain/range checks and six competency questions, with six authored queries scored under seven fixed strategies. The retained \texttt{S2-typed-lexical-loose} strategy recovered 6/6 authored holdouts ($P=R=F_1=1.000$, realistic-tie $\mathrm{MRR}=0.944$, Brier $=0.131$, $\mathrm{Sem}@3=1.000$); the degree baseline recovered none. These are construction results, not OWL/SHACL conformance, semantic completeness, runtime retrieval, or support for any \texttt{C-RESULT-*} claim.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{Established Scope}
The designed pilot establishes mechanism behavior only for its frozen cases: encoded checks isolate state, bounded repair and fallback follow their declared paths, named corruptions are rejected by their designated operations, and assigned rows remain classified. It does not attribute a rejection to a stable detector code.

The guided-versus-oracle boundary is the honest reading of the offline repair comparison: the state-reading callback converts the oracle-only case that $\rho$ by construction cannot, while $\rho$ matches the oracle on the guided-repairable class using only the validator's counterexamples. The live extension separated $\rho$ from blind retry only when the constructed state placed every proposal in the guided-repairable class; already-valid and guided-irreparable regimes were null, so regime dependence, the unpinned hosted revision, and quasi-replicated calls block any general sample-efficiency or model-capability claim. Feedback-consuming repair loops are precedented: Self-Refine feeds back language generated by the same untrusted model \cite{S45_madaan2023selfrefine}, Reflexion stores verbal self-reflections across trials \cite{S48_shinn2023reflexion}, and Self-Debugging consumes execution results from a trusted full-program runtime \cite{S46_chen2023selfdebug}. $\rho$ differs in channel and authority: its feedback is a typed error set computed by the deterministic validator before execution, and every failed live arm remains state-isolated. Its name follows counterexample-guided inductive synthesis, in which a verifier's counterexample rather than the full specification state drives the next candidate \cite{S47_solarlezama2006sketching}; here the verifier is the commit gate itself.

\subsection{Relationship to Prior Work}
\system{} complements rather than replaces constrained decoders \cite{S09_geng2023grammar,S10_beurerkellner2023prompting,S36_scholak2021picard}, retrieval and memory components \cite{S04_he2024gretriever,S05_gutierrez2024hipporag,S06_chhikara2025mem0}, and executable environments and benchmarks \cite{S11_cote2019textworld,S12_wang2022scienceworld,S15_liu2024agentbench,S18_paglieri2025balrog}; process-linked traces instantiate AgentBoard's measurement concern \cite{S16_ma2024agentboard}, not evidence of outperformance. The confirmatory studies---a preregistered multi-model comparison with an independent semantic oracle and a genuine blind-retry comparator, retrieval and memory ablations at matched encoded validity, and a blinded matched-validity human study with justified power and mixed-effects analysis---remain outside this paper; the screening pilot substitutes for none of them \cite{S15_liu2024agentbench,S27_vanderlee2019best,S31_agarwal2021precipice,S35_barr2013random}.

\section{Threats, Ethics, and Artifact Scope}
The evidence has nine explicit boundaries. \textbf{L1:} encoded predicates cannot detect semantics omitted from structured fields, and state consistency alone is not behavioral or narrative coherence \cite{S55_huang2026beyond}. \textbf{L2:} one authored world and one constructed live state do not establish cross-world, cross-policy, or scale generalization. \textbf{L3:} the state-reading callback is an oracle; offline classes and regime-dependent screening do not establish general repair quality or sample efficiency. \textbf{L4:} the single hosted proposer has an unpinned revision, no token accounting, and uncontrolled seeds; no live Python--Godot round trip exists. \textbf{L5:} no participant or affect data exist. \textbf{L6:} unkeyed hashes give integrity, not writer authentication or tamper resistance. \textbf{L7:} record consistency assumes one writer. \textbf{L8:} engine evidence is one Godot fixture with startup-dominated samples, not a portability or real-time result. \textbf{L9:} selected fixtures and screening cells support no population, safety-rate, or model-ranking inference. Internal validity also assumes independent policy expectations and no fixture leakage.

The pilot processes no participants or personal telemetry; future human and affect work requires ethics review, informed consent, minimization, retention rules, and opt-out. LLM judges, if used, remain auxiliary because automated and crowd judgements both require calibration and bias controls \cite{S27_vanderlee2019best,S28_karpinska2021perils,S29_zheng2023judging,S30_chen2024judgement}.

\section{Conclusion}
\system{} isolates candidate-event payloads from canonical mutation through an encoded symbolic commit gate and makes terminal outcomes auditable through linked records and state-semantic replay. The offline pilot establishes mechanism conformance on frozen fixtures; the live screening pilot separated $\rho$ from blind retry (5/5 versus 0/5) only in the constructed repairable regime and remains pilot-only. Cross-model superiority, player-experience benefit, retrieval or memory effectiveness, affect efficacy, and commercial-engine performance remain confirmatory extensions.
