\begin{abstract}
\system{} treats generated game events as untrusted proposals. Strict parsing, external policy, seven predicates in six families, bounded repair, and pre-commit revalidation gate mutation; typed-error $\rho$ reads candidates/errors, not authoritative state. Unkeyed SHA-256 links, semantic replay, and complete accounting audit outcomes. Separate evidence covers one-world offline mechanism conformance, single-model live screening, non-authoritative closed-world graph simulation, and participant-free Godot engineering. At $K{=}1$, guided/blind commits differed 5/5 versus 0/5 only in a constructed repairable policy-blind regime. No lane establishes population efficacy, model superiority, player experience, or commercial-engine performance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem Definition and Guarantee Boundary}
$c_t=(G_t,q_t)$ stores typed snapshot $G_t$ and external action-policy/disclosure/quest-stage constraints $q_t$. Predicates neither read $h_{<t}$ nor reconstruct \texttt{WorldState} from logs. Transitions update $G_t$, not $q_t$; policy rotation is excluded. Retrieval, scores, rationales, memory, future affect have no authority.

Strict parsing of untrusted candidate $a_t$ enforces the shared key/schema contract, rejecting unknown top-level fields. Validation returns
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
V(c_t,a_t)&=(\boldsymbol{v},E_t),\\
\boldsymbol{v}&=(v_{\mathrm{policy}},v_{\mathrm{pre}},v_{\mathrm{reach}},v_{\mathrm{know}},\\
&\qquad v_{\mathrm{disc}},v_{\mathrm{elig}},v_{\mathrm{stage}})\in\{0,1\}^{7},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $E_t$ is a structured error set. Mutation is
\begin{equation}
c_{t+1}=\begin{cases}
T(c_t,a_t), & \bigwedge_{i=1}^{7}(v_i=1),\\
c_t, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:transition}
\end{equation}

Fixtures test implementation invariants, not theorems. \textbf{I1:} validation exhaustion or pre-commit proposal/controller failure preserves state. \textbf{I2:} returning callbacks and $K\geq0$ bound recorded attempts to $K+1$, plus pre-commit revalidation; without enforced deadlines, wall-clock termination is not guaranteed. \textbf{I3:} accepted \texttt{CandidateAction.effects} stay within \texttt{ActionPolicy.allowed\_effects}, including authorized quest-stage targets. \textbf{I4:} frozen schemas/deterministic transitions enable terminal-state reconstruction.

Assumptions: correct policy, faithful extraction, deterministic versions, returning callbacks, single-process file ownership. Replay checks structured transitions, not repair provenance, prompts, stochastic generation, or undeclared natural-language implications.

Repair cannot mutate state; exhaustion preserves it. Typed \texttt{counterexample\_guided\_repair} reads only candidate $a$/errors $E$ (its sole state-derived input), never \texttt{WorldState}:
\begin{equation}
\rho(a,E)=a\oplus\bigcup_{e\in E}\delta(e),
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
Table~\ref{tab:rho-rules}'s $\oplus$ applies deterministic $\delta(e)$: one field/error entity, at most $|E|$/attempt; conflicting requirements/rejections leave fields unchanged. Fixed points require identical $E$, not convergence. The nondeployable state-reading oracle ignores errors, reconstructs preconditions/effects/both quest-stage fields from state, and never launders violations by deleting required objects/used facts/disclosed facts.

\subsection{Integrity, Semantic Replay, and Accounting}\label{sec:audit}
Unkeyed SHA-256 checksums/trace links detect accidental/tested changes, not writer identity or joint content/checksum rewriting. Replay strictly parses prior state/candidate/validation, requires invalid nonfinal attempts, recomputes final state, checks equality/adjacent JSONL continuity, and never reruns repair. Request$\rightarrow$decision$\rightarrow$result and first-issued-action/exactly-one-receipt binding prohibit neither duplicate-action-ID issuance nor impersonation.

Commit, symbolic fallback, adapter failure, controller failure are terminal classes; controller failure preserves state/assignment without a completed proposal trace. Pre-execution keys bind run, arm, scenario, seed, model, revision, configuration/input/prior-state hashes. Aggregation rejects duplicate/missing/unexpected keys and separates failures.

\section{Discussion, Threats, Ethics, and Artifact Scope}
