\begin{abstract}
Generated game events can satisfy a schema and cite known facts while requesting inaccessible objects, unauthorized state changes, or premature disclosure. \system{} separates proposal authority from commit authority: an external deterministic gate checks the encoded state and authored policy, supplies typed errors for repair, and revalidates the final candidate. Transition records support replay and assignment-complete accounting. A source-bound bilingual ontology view exposes these decisions without granting write access.

The deterministic audit crosses 32 graph/state combinations with 13 candidate variants, assigning 416 cases to each of four arms. Against an implementation-separated reference, the schema-only control committed 260/416 reference-invalid final candidates; each semantic-gate arm had 0/416 reference-invalid final commits. Guided repair recovered 128/260 initially invalid proposals into reference-valid commits and retained all 11/11 initially valid structural signatures. Reachability closure and breadth-first search agreed on 32/32 graph/states. In paired sentinels, six authored undeclared paraphrases passed the structured semantic gates, while six counterparts explicitly declaring forbidden facts were held. This contrast identifies a declared-field coverage limit, not a natural-language safety rate. The evidence covers authored finite reusable-key graphs and implementations that share encoded rules, not independently validated ground truth or language-model or human efficacy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{G}{enerated} events must satisfy a game's data contract and current rules. A proposal may require an item inside a locked room, disclose a fact the NPC knows but may not yet reveal, or perform a permitted action that does not advance the objective. Grounding and plausible dialogue \cite{S03_weir2024ontologically,S20_ashby2023personalized} supply content; the game must still decide which state changes to allow.



\system{} enforces this decision at a proposal-to-commit boundary (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}). Models and adapters submit typed candidates. The external deterministic gate checks preconditions, object reachability, NPC knowledge, disclosure permission, effects, and quest-stage constraints. Failed checks return structured repair information. Repair changes the candidate, not the state; revalidation against the unchanged state and policy authorizes a commit. The read-only ontology view links source declarations to checks and public decisions. Replay and assignment records preserve the resulting execution path.
The research question is which checks distinguish a well-formed proposal, an authorized transition, and an achieved objective, and which records support each decision.

In v5, ten of 20 assigned responses passed parsing, eight passed the semantic gate, and seven reached the goal: authorization did not imply objective success. Ordinary-rule and ontology-gate implementations agreed on 60/60 cases under frozen disclosure rules. The deterministic audit in Sec.~\ref{sec:review-boundary-audit} tests a schema-only intervention and a separated reference, recording proposal--repair--hold outcomes across a finite graph family.

\section{Related Work and Research Gap}

\section{Problem Definition and Guarantee Boundary}
In $c_t=(G_t,q_t)$, $G_t$ is the typed game snapshot and $q_t$ contains externally authored action-policy, disclosure, and quest-stage constraints. Python carries both in \texttt{WorldState}: locations, reachable locations, object locations, inventory, facts, NPC knowledge, and quest stage describe the state; action policies and forbidden disclosures constrain it. An \texttt{ActionPolicy} specifies required preconditions, allowed and required effects, and allowed quest-stage targets. These are two conceptual roles, not independently stored Python objects. Predicates neither read $h_{<t}$ nor reconstruct \texttt{WorldState}. Transitions update $G_t$ only and exclude policy rotation. Retrieval, scores, rationales, memory, and future affect have no mutation authority.

After strict parsing of an untrusted proposal $a_t$, the validator runs seven deterministic predicates and returns \texttt{ValidationResult(valid, errors, checks\_run)}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
V(c_t,a_t)&=(\mathrm{valid},E_t,C_{\mathrm{run}}),\\
\boldsymbol{v}&=(v_{\mathrm{policy}},v_{\mathrm{pre}},v_{\mathrm{reach}},v_{\mathrm{know}},\\
&\qquad v_{\mathrm{disc}},v_{\mathrm{elig}},v_{\mathrm{stage}})\in\{0,1\}^{7},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $E_t$ contains structured errors with code, entity, reason, and repair hint; $C_{\mathrm{run}}$ lists the seven executed check identifiers. The aggregate $\mathrm{valid}\equiv\bigwedge_{i=1}^{7}(v_i=1)$ summarizes the seven predicate outcomes: policy, precondition, reachability, NPC knowledge, forbidden disclosure, stage eligibility, and stage monotonicity. The runtime returns \texttt{valid} and check identifiers, not seven individual Booleans; an absent per-check trace or omitted field remains unobserved. The transition below applies to the current candidate $a_t$, which repair may have changed, rather than the original proposal's entire transaction. Its $\boldsymbol{v}$ checks use unchanged $c_t$. Rejection leaves $c_t$ unchanged while bounded repair or fallback continues; passing same-policy revalidation authorizes mutation:
\begin{equation}
c_{t+1}=\begin{cases}
T(c_t,a_t), & \bigwedge_{i=1}^{7}(v_i=1),\\
c_t, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:transition}
\end{equation}

Execution follows proposal $\rightarrow$ deterministic validation $\rightarrow$ observable validity, check identifiers and structured errors $\rightarrow$ optional bounded repair $\rightarrow$ same-state revalidation $\rightarrow$ commit or unchanged fallback. A candidate is checked again immediately before application; repair exhaustion or an absent repairer leaves state unchanged. The guided operator uses only the proposal and errors. The general Python callback also receives \texttt{WorldState}, so the restricted-input claim does not cover every callback. A controlled comparison must give deterministic and model-based detailed repair the same proposal and errors; the same model's generic-error condition receives a generic signal. This design alone implies no comparison outcome.

The fixtures test invariants, not theorems. \textbf{I1:} validation exhaustion or pre-commit proposal/controller failure preserves state. \textbf{I2:} returning callbacks and $K\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$ bound attempts to $K+1$ with pre-commit revalidation; without deadlines, wall-clock termination is not guaranteed. \textbf{I3:} accepted \texttt{CandidateAction.effects} stay within \texttt{ActionPolicy.allowed\_effects}, including authorized quest-stage targets. \textbf{I4:} a recorded initial snapshot, complete ordered transition record, and frozen schema and deterministic transition version permit reconstruction of the recorded terminal structured state. Reconstruction does not authenticate the record, establish semantic correctness, or reproduce model generation.

These invariants assume correct policy/extraction, deterministic versions, returning callbacks, and single-process ownership. Replay checks structured transitions, not repair provenance, prompts, stochastic generation, or undeclared natural-language implications.

\subsection{Schema Validity, Authorization, and Goal Success}\label{sec:authorization-sets}
Fix the encoded pre-action state $c_t$, authored policy, and strict candidate schema. Let $\mathcal{S}$ contain schema-valid candidates; define authorization and objective achievement separately:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{A}(c_t)&=\{a\in\mathcal{S}:V(c_t,a).\mathrm{valid}\},\\
\mathcal{G}(c_t)&=\{a\in\mathcal{S}:\operatorname{Goal}(T(c_t,a))\},\\
\mathcal{H}(c_t)&=\mathcal{A}(c_t)\cap\mathcal{G}(c_t),\\
\mathcal{H}(c_t)&\subseteq\mathcal{A}(c_t)\subseteq\mathcal{S}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The set $\mathcal{G}$ includes candidates with a defined encoded transition $T$; evaluating that transition does not authorize execution. By definition, $\mathcal{H}$ requires both authorization and goal attainment, and authorization requires schema validity. A schema-valid forbidden-fact declaration can fall outside $\mathcal{A}$. A permitted \texttt{observe} action can fall inside $\mathcal{A}$ but outside $\mathcal{H}$ if it leaves the goal false. Neither $\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{G}$ nor $\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{A}$ follows in general. This conditional set argument is not a new verification theorem: it assumes faithful state/policy extraction, correct predicates and transitions, and an unchanged pre-action snapshot. Strict parsing alone cannot establish these state-dependent conditions. We report no XGrammar execution or token-masking comparison.

Knowledge and disclosure permission are separate inputs. In the audit, the NPC knows \texttt{keeper\_secret} at both stages, but disclosure is forbidden at stage $0$ and permitted at stage $1$. Permission changes without adding knowledge. Retrieval, rationale, affect, style, and personality scores cannot override this policy.

\rho(a,E)=a\oplus\bigcup_{e\in E}\delta(e),

\subsection{Integrity, Semantic Replay, and Accounting}\label{sec:audit}
Unkeyed SHA-256 detects accidental and tested changes, not identity or coordinated rewriting. Replay checks the prior state, candidate, validation, invalid nonfinal attempts, recomputed final state, and JSONL continuity; it does not replay repair generation. Request$\rightarrow$decision$\rightarrow$result and first-action/one-receipt bindings do not prevent duplicate IDs or impersonation. Terminal classes are commit, symbolic fallback, adapter failure, and controller failure; failures preserve state/assignment without a completed proposal trace. Pre-execution run/arm/scenario/seed/model/revision keys and configuration/input/prior-state hashes bind assignments. Aggregation rejects duplicate, missing, or unexpected keys and reports failures separately.

\textbf{Two reproducibility layers.} First, local checks verify frozen receipt schemas, assignment accounting, source/input hash bindings, encoded transition semantics, and replay under specified contracts. They require neither new model responses nor a network listener. Their results apply to the artifacts and properties actually checked, not every table, binary, or platform. Bitwise identity requires support from the exact source, inputs, toolchain, and compared bytes. Matching archived hashes does not establish identical regeneration of provider responses or build outputs across machines.

Second, recollecting v5 or native-model episodes requires a separate experiment with model-admission and response-identity evidence, authorized access, budget, deadlines, and a frozen protocol. Local receipt/replay checks cannot supply missing served-model identity or recover unretained CLI output. No provider recollection was performed for this revision. Deterministic accounting and replay therefore do not imply universal portability or bitwise reproduction of historical model collections.

The public review bundle withholds the private Gemini conversation URL and original reviewer-source bytes. Under \path{research/academic-pipeline/gemini-review-20261113/}, \path{reviewer-source.public.json} and \path{public-source-projection-v1.json} identify the sanitized derivative. Run \texttt{python -B }\path{scripts/verify_paper_claims.py}\allowbreak\ \texttt{--public-source-projection} to check that derivative, frozen results, and other original-hash commitments. \subsection{Deterministic Authorization-Boundary Audit}\label{sec:review-boundary-audit}
The frozen \path{research/academic-pipeline/gemini-review-20261113/protocol-v2.json} defines the designer-authored offline audit separately from E1, NPC-DISC-1, v5, and the prospective pilot. It crosses two room counts (4, 8), two topologies (locked chain, unlocked bypass branch), four start/goal placements of two keys, and two quest stages: $2\times2\times4\times2=32$ graph/state combinations. Thirteen candidate variants per combination yield 416 assignments per arm. Variants include permitted signal/observe actions and targeted policy, knowledge, disclosure, stage, object, and precondition violations. These counts specify the design, not observed successes or stochastic replicates.
The 1,664 main assignments cross these authored states, variants, and arms; they are not independent samples from a game or model population. The 48 surface assignments have a separate denominator.

\textbf{Graph and reachability.} Typed records specify rooms, a start room, bidirectional edges with required-key sets, and object-to-room placements. Each chain has two keyed boundaries; an unlocked bypass routes around the first. A monotone closure supplies production \texttt{WorldState.reachable\_locations}; a separately implemented standard-library breadth-first search examines (room, collected reusable keys). Both assume finite undirected graphs with persistent, reusable keys collected on visits. The profile excludes key consumption, random transitions, dynamic edges, inventory limits, and irreversible branches. Entity names and generated prose cannot supply missing edges or placements. Agreement checks reachability within this profile, not full-game playability.

\textbf{Interventions and reference.} The \texttt{schema\_only} arm ($K{=}0$) uses the strict shared parser, bypasses semantic gating, and applies parsed effects/stage in an isolated simulator. This is an executed intervention, unlike E1's derived schema-only counterfactual. \texttt{semantic\_rejection} uses $K{=}0$; \texttt{unchanged\_retry} and \texttt{guided\_repair} use $K{=}1$. The reference extracts the existing ordinary-rule predicate and implements BFS without importing the production validator, runtime, repair operator, or closure. It evaluates initial and repaired final candidates against the same immutable \emph{pre-action} state, differing from the production state only in BFS-derived reachability. Goal evaluation uses the resulting state. This implementation separation reduces gate-as-evaluator circularity; it is not independently authored or blinded ground truth.

\textbf{Funnel and retention.} Pre-gate invalidity divides reference-invalid initial candidates by all assignments. Repair recovery divides initially invalid cases ending in a reference-valid commit by all initially invalid cases, including in the $K{=}0$ controls. Holds and failures each use all assignments and are reported separately. Unsafe commits use both assigned and committed denominators. False rejection divides initially reference-valid cases ending in hold by all initially reference-valid cases. Goals use all assignments. Exact structural retention divides distinct initially valid signatures preserved unchanged in a reference-valid commit by all distinct initially valid signatures; case-level preservation is also reported. Signatures include action type, preconditions/effects, required objects, used/disclosed facts, and both quest-stage fields, but exclude IDs, prose, metadata, locations, and graph labels. Distinct initially invalid and valid repaired signatures are counted separately. These measures assess encoded structure, not lexical diversity, creativity, or player-rated quality.

\section{Discussion, Threats, Ethics, and Artifact Scope}
The guided semantic repair operator $\rho$ \cite{S45_madaan2023selfrefine,S48_shinn2023reflexion,S46_chen2023selfdebug,S47_solarlezama2006sketching} uses deterministic pre-execution errors; the oracle-only arm reads state. Rule agreement and edit-scope diagnostics establish neither model capability nor ontology superiority.
Controller versions confound the native comparison: row 1 failed under controller-v3 before a response, whereas rows 2--8 used controller-v4 after a one-line JSON-boundary correction. Model-visible inputs were unchanged, but selector/representation differences cannot be attributed to the model.


The fixed-game pilot provides bounded native evidence, not ontology efficacy. A (model/stack consistency) and C (fresh-reference noninferiority) remain unexecuted. They require budget-bounded completion under fixed source/policy, sealed assignments, exact identity/revision fields, and separate safety/replay accounting; the pilot answers neither. The proposed endpoint runs from request/evidence receipt to the first receipt passing frozen mandatory checks (time-to-first-all-predeclared-validation-pass). Unfinished, timeout, failure, and stage-not-reached runs have no successful time.
The proposed endpoint is measured in continuous task-clock seconds.

The identity-required extension stopped at missing served-model evidence (Appendix~\ref{app:runtime-identity}); no new identity-qualified comparison was obtained.

The deterministic audit covers more authored graph/states, not more sampled models or players. Reusable-key reachability does not validate omitted mechanics; a shared rule specification can cause shared reference/production errors. Exact structural retention measures neither linguistic variety nor voice stability. Authored paraphrase pairs test a declared-field boundary, not prevalence or unrestricted-dialogue safety. No new admitted multi-model or human efficacy collection was performed.
The v5 collection's 20 assignments, the native pilot's eight assignments, and NPC-DISC-1's 60 authored cases measure distinct constructs with distinct denominators. Pooling them with the 1,664 deterministic case assignments or 48 surface assignments would not create a population sample. All five C-RESULT outcomes remain \texttt{TODO-RESULT}; TR01--TR06 remain unmeasured, and prospective execution remains 0/180.

\textbf{Threats and boundaries.} The native pilot has one authored quest and two repeats per cell, without comparator power, neutral complete-attempt inventories, or independent traceability scores. State consistency does not establish behavioral consistency \cite{S55_huang2026beyond} or narrative coherence. Predicate/role/Godot/DISC sets are not latent states; declarations are not human gold or holdouts; evidence lanes have separate denominators. The data support no participant, affect, comprehension, agency, enjoyment, immersion, benefit, population, safety-rate, model-ranking, or commercial-performance inference. Hashes do not authenticate, and replay equality is not semantic correctness. Future human/affect studies require ethics review, consent, minimization, retention/deletion, and opt-out. Prior evaluation work motivates compensation disclosure and calibrated judges \cite{S27_vanderlee2019best,S28_karpinska2021perils,S29_zheng2023judging,S30_chen2024judgement}.
