Five Phase-3 findings and protocol boundaries emerge cleanly from Table~\ref{tab:phase3-supplements}:
(1) Across all three model families (\textbf{P6}), fixed 3-turn choreography yields 88/90 (97.8\%) parsed turns and confirms zero-leak protection on 30/30 episodes under multi-hop shielding (vs.\ 14/30 flat, $p{=}3.05{\times}10^{-5}$), while $\rho_{K=2}$ raises safe milestones from 7/30 (23.33\%) to 16/30 (53.33\%, $p{=}0.00391$); notably, \texttt{gpt-oss-120b-medium} emitted an invalid placeholder \texttt{action\_type} on all 9 parsed Turn-1 attempts (1 PF), contributing 0 commits and 0 flat unsafe leaks via fail-closed Turn-1 rejection.
(2) On 90 live 5-world proposals (\textbf{P7}), cross-family parse reliability reaches 87/90 (96.7\%: Claude 30/30, Hydrogen 30/30, GPT-OSS 27/30), with multi-hop shielding holding unsafe commits to 0/87 vs.\ 27/87 flat and deterministic $\rho_{K=2}$ repairing 30/30 noisy-policy failures at zero extra tokens ($Q{=}191.28$); because our stateless $K{=}1$ LLM regeneration condition omitted the failed candidate JSON $a$ from the retry prompt (causing 20/20 actor/\texttt{used\_facts} resets in Claude and Hydrogen), its 1/30 recovery at 1,717,068 tokens measures candidate-blind regeneration rather than an apples-to-apples stateful repair baseline.
(3) Across 180 unseen EN/KO utterances evaluated by three neural verifiers spanning two families (Anthropic and Google Gemini; 540 matrix calls after \texttt{gpt-oss} hit HTTP~429, \textbf{P8}), agreement reaches Fleiss' $\kappa{=}0.9614$ and Krippendorff's $\alpha_{\mathrm{nominal}}{=}0.9614$ ($Q{=}2.80, p{=}0.2466$), with Claude achieving 72/72 paraphrase recall at 3/72 (4.17\%) benign false rejections ($F_1{=}0.9863$) vs.\ Hydrogen's 72/72 and 0/72 ($F_1{=}1.000$); human \texttt{TR01--TR06} verification remains $N{=}0$.
(4) Bottom-up Datalog scaling across 480 cases up to $R{=}64$ rooms and closure depth $d{=}5$ (\textbf{P9}) verifies 480/480 oracle agreement: \texttt{flat\_}\allowbreak\texttt{1hop\_}\allowbreak\texttt{gate} drops to 46.67\% at $d{\ge}2$ and \texttt{bounded\_}\allowbreak\texttt{2hop\_}\allowbreak\texttt{gate} drops to 60.00\% at $d{\ge}3$, while arbitrary-depth transitive closure sustains 100.0\% at $R$-invariant 6.46--7.12\,$\mu$s ($O(d)$ closure cost, $Q{=}1100.19$).
(5) Across 50 live step-by-step episodes inside compiled Godot~4.7.1 across all five state-machine worlds (\textbf{P10}), Cohort~A empirically validates Theorem~2's anti-laundering block (30/30 safe replays, 0/30 leaks, 1/30 goals without \texttt{npc\_knowledge}), whereas policy/knowledge-guided Cohort~B confirms 20/20 (100.0\%) end-to-end Godot~4.7.1 execution and 10/10 shorthand $\rho$ repairs when target actions, \texttt{actor\_npc\_knowledge}, and initial shorthand omissions are specified in the prompt, with 50/50 zero-LLM replay parity.
