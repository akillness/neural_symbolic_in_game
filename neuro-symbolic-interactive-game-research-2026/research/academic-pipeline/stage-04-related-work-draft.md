# II. Related Work and Research Gap

## A. LLM-Based Interactive Narrative and Game Agents

Language models have expanded interactive narrative from selecting authored branches toward generating quests, dialogue, and agent behavior at run time. KNUDGE formalizes one important part of this problem: branching NPC dialogue grounded in quest and entity specifications extracted from a commercial role-playing game \cite{S03_weir2024ontologically}. Personalized quest and dialogue generation likewise combines a knowledge graph with a language model, illustrating how structured world knowledge can condition authored experiences \cite{S20_ashby2023personalized}. Generative Agents adds observation, memory, reflection, and planning to produce believable social behavior in an interactive sandbox \cite{S07_park2023generative}, while Character-LLM focuses on role profiles, experiences, and character-conditioned behavior \cite{S08_shao2023character}. These systems demonstrate complementary mechanisms for grounded dialogue, personalization, and role consistency, but their reported objectives do not by themselves establish that a proposed action is executable or authorized in a particular canonical game state.

Player-facing studies further show why symbolic correctness cannot stand in for experience quality. Akoury *et al.* combine grounded generation with player preference and qualitative evaluation in commercial-game contexts \cite{S21_akoury2023player}, and recent IUI work reports player responses to generative NPC dialogue beyond predefined scripts \cite{S22_hochreiter2026beyond}. Yin *et al.* study contextualized generation and player experience in an online record assigned to a 2026 issue; its final issue metadata therefore remains conditional and must be rechecked before submission \cite{S23_yin2026contextualized}. A 2025 preprint additionally reports role-dependent trade-offs between constraint-induced stability and improvisational believability \cite{S02_figueiredo2025scaffolded}. We use that preprint only as recent motivation, not as the sole basis for a central claim. Together, this literature motivates separating hard game-state admissibility from optional assessments of naturalness, personalization, and player preference.

Player-adaptive generation has also been framed through experience-driven procedural content generation \cite{S24_yannakakis2011experiencedriven}, while observer-based work operationalizes moment-to-moment engagement labels from gameplay streams \cite{S25_melhart2020engagement}. A recent 2026 preprint reports limitations in vision-language-model inference of latent engagement from gameplay cues \cite{S26_wang2026engagement}. Because S26 is non-archival and engagement observations are not canonical game facts, this line informs only TRACE-RPG's future, non-authoritative affect track; it supplies no evidence for the present transaction mechanism.

## B. Interactive Environments and Game-Agent Benchmarks

Executable environments expose failures that static language evaluation can conceal. TextWorld generates text-based games with explicit state and supports held-out evaluation \cite{S11_cote2019textworld}; ScienceWorld evaluates whether agents can apply scientific knowledge through grounded interaction rather than merely answer questions \cite{S12_wang2022scienceworld}. MineDojo extends evaluation to open-ended, knowledge-rich embodied tasks \cite{S13_fan2022minedojo}. At a broader methodological level, the General Video Game AI framework separates agents, games, and content-generation algorithms into distinct tracks \cite{S14_perezliebana2019gvgai}, a distinction reflected in TRACE-RPG's separation of research runtime, game bridge, and generated content.

Recent agent benchmarks emphasize heterogeneity and longitudinal process. AgentBench evaluates language-model agents across multiple interactive environments and analyzes recurrent failure modes \cite{S15_liu2024agentbench}. AgentBoard supplements terminal success with progress-oriented diagnostics for multi-turn agents \cite{S16_ma2024agentboard}. SOTOPIA evaluates social goals, cooperation, competition, and role-play \cite{S17_zhou2024sotopia}, whereas BALROG focuses on long-horizon reasoning across games and both language and visual interfaces \cite{S18_paglieri2025balrog}. Automated human-like play-style generation provides a complementary precedent for production-oriented game testing, although automated agents do not replace player studies \cite{S19_lepelletier2022playtesting}. These benchmarks motivate multitrack scenarios, explicit failure taxonomies, and process traces, but their aggregate scores do not specify a transactional authorization boundary for generated state changes.

## C. Constrained and Structured Generation

Constrained decoding can improve the structural reliability of model outputs. Grammar-constrained decoding generalizes formal output restrictions across structured NLP tasks \cite{S09_geng2023grammar}; LMQL combines prompting, constraints, and control flow in a model-independent query language \cite{S10_beurerkellner2023prompting}; and PICARD incrementally rejects inadmissible token continuations during structured generation \cite{S36_scholak2021picard}. These methods provide strong comparators for schema-valid candidate production and bounded control flow. However, syntactic admissibility is distinct from state-relative authorization. A well-formed event can still omit a prerequisite, reference an unreachable object, disclose knowledge unavailable to an NPC, introduce an effect outside the action policy, or regress a quest stage. TRACE-RPG therefore treats constrained decoding as an upstream proposal aid and rechecks every candidate against an externally supplied canonical policy before mutation. This is a boundary between generation and authorization, not a claim that prior constrained decoders are deficient for their intended tasks.

## D. Neuro-Symbolic Validation, Graph Retrieval, and Memory

The closest game-specific neuro-symbolic comparator is IVIE, which incrementally generates interactive-fiction worlds while applying symbolic validation \cite{S01_vaucher2026ivie}. At the time of this review, IVIE was available as a preprint associated with accepted/forthcoming, non-archival ICCC 2026 material; its publication status is therefore not overstated here. Its reported validation gaps also illustrate that an encoded symbolic layer is only as complete as its objectives and predicates. TRACE-RPG accordingly limits its guarantee to the predicates represented in the tested transition domain.

Graph retrieval and long-term memory address a different problem: selecting and retaining relevant evidence. G-Retriever retrieves explanatory subgraphs from large textual graphs \cite{S04_he2024gretriever}; HippoRAG combines knowledge graphs with Personalized PageRank for long-term, multi-hop retrieval \cite{S05_gutierrez2024hipporag}; and Mem0 extracts, consolidates, and retrieves conversational memories, including a graph-based variant \cite{S06_chhikara2025mem0}. Such components can condition future proposals and support temporal analyses, but retrieval relevance does not authorize canonical mutation. TRACE-RPG therefore places graph retrieval and memory outside the commit boundary. Their effects on contradiction rates remain future ablations rather than results of the present deterministic pilot.

## E. Event-Sourced Auditing and Reproducible Evaluation

Reliable game-agent claims require more than a final score. AgentBoard's process metrics motivate retaining intermediate progress and failure information \cite{S16_ma2024agentboard}, while robust-evaluation work warns that sparse seeds and aggregate means can obscure variability \cite{S31_agarwal2021precipice}. Reproducibility guidance calls for disclosing code, data, environments, and seeds \cite{S33_pineau2021reproducibility}; energy-reporting guidance further requires clear hardware and measurement boundaries \cite{S34_henderson2020energy}. Human and automated judges add separate validity concerns: best-practice guidance emphasizes explicit constructs, instructions, raters, and reliability \cite{S27_vanderlee2019best}, and studies of crowd and LLM judging document population and bias risks \cite{S28_karpinska2021perils,S29_zheng2023judging,S30_chen2024judgement}. Future non-inferiority and participant studies additionally require justified equivalence margins and crossed random-effects structures rather than arbitrary thresholds or independent-observation assumptions \cite{S32_lakens2018equivalence,S35_barr2013random}. These sources support TRACE-RPG's failure-complete records and bounded reporting, but none is treated as evidence that its implementation is correct.

Within the approved evidence pool, individual works cover executable environments, grounded dialogue, structural constraints, symbolic validation, retrieval, memory, process metrics, or reproducibility. Our novelty claim is deliberately narrower than asserting that any one component is new or that no prior system could contain a similar mechanism. TRACE-RPG implements these concerns as an **integrated auditable transaction protocol**: an untrusted proposal is parsed into a typed event, checked against state-relative hard predicates and an externally supplied action policy, optionally repaired within a fixed budget, committed only after revalidation, and linked to assignment-complete records that support checksum verification, semantic replay, and episode-continuity checks. Parsing is type-strict for known fields but currently ignores unknown top-level candidate fields. The present paper evaluates conformance of this implemented combination in frozen offline fixtures; it does not infer live-model superiority, improved player experience, temporal-memory benefit, affect efficacy, or commercial-engine portability.
